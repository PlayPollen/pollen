## Why the split?

An open-source code license (AGPL) lets anyone read, learn from, and contribute
to the code — which is the point of an open community project. But it would also
let anyone ship *your game*, art and all, under a different skin. Keeping assets
under a separate proprietary license means:

- The **code** is genuinely open and contributable.
- Your **game as a product** — its look, sound, and name — stays yours.
- A fork gets working farming logic, but not *this* game.

## For contributors

If you contribute assets, you agree they may be included under this asset
license (not AGPL) unless we agree otherwise in writing. If you want your
contributed art to stay under, say, a Creative Commons license, note it in your
PR and we'll track it in `assets/CREDITS.md`.
